Start Here — RR Administrators

You are the person who keeps RapidReconciler trustworthy for everyone else — the right people have access, the data is current, and the service is healthy. This guide walks you through every one of those jobs in plain language.

When you sign in, RapidReconciler opens on your Home screen. At the top, Set Context fixes the database, companies, and period you’re working in. Below that, your administrator surface leads with the RapidReconciler AI band — tap a suggested question and RapidReconciler answers from your own instance — over four cards you act on: Licensing, Team & access, Report Engine, and Activity log. A quiet instance-health strip underneath carries the background checks. The Your Home Dashboard topics in the sidebar walk through each one, with pictures of exactly what you’ll see.

Your Responsibilities

Four things are yours to keep true: the right people have the right access, the data is current, capacity and licensing stay ahead of demand, and the engine is healthy. Most of these run on a built-in reminder, and the RapidReconciler AI band on Home rolls up whatever is due — ask What needs my attention right now? — so this is what you own, not a list you have to keep in your head.

Your First Setup

The recommended order to configure RapidReconciler for a first deployment. Each step links into the procedural topic that explains how to do it. Following the order matters — some steps depend on earlier ones.

Recommended Sequence

#ActionWhy This Order
1 Read About Administration. Confirms how the administrator role is assigned and gives you the vocabulary for the rest of the setup.
2 Configure each licensed company. See Company Management. Fiscal year start, base currency, AAI document types, tolerance thresholds. Everything downstream filters by company, so getting this right first prevents rework later.
3 Add users and assign access. See Manage the Team. Users are added after companies because per-module company access depends on companies being configured first. Adding users earlier means revisiting them after company setup.
4 Walk the Pre-launch Checklist before announcing availability to end-users. Confirms each area is set up correctly and that a representative end-user can productively work in the application before broader rollout.

If You Are Adding a Company After Initial Deployment

The sequence above describes a first deployment. Once RapidReconciler is live and you need to add a newly-licensed JDE company:

  1. Submit the company licensing request to GSI support — see Adding a Company License.
  2. Once GSI confirms the license is in place, configure the new company per Company Management.
  3. Update existing users so they can see the new company — users set to All licensed companies pick it up automatically; users on a specific company list must be edited to include it.

If You Are Inheriting an Existing Deployment

If you are taking over administration of a RapidReconciler instance that someone else configured:

  • Walk the Pre-launch Checklist as a sanity check — treat it as a configuration audit rather than a setup procedure.
  • Pay particular attention to user access lists. Stale users (departed staff still configured) are the most common finding when an administrator changes hands.
When you’re done with the sequence, walk the Pre-launch Checklist to confirm the deployment is ready for end-users.
Try this now

For new deployments: identify which step from the recommended sequence you’re currently on and what’s blocking the next one. The most common blockers are waiting for company licensing from GSI or waiting for end-user lists from the customer’s HR or operations team. Knowing where you’re stuck is the first step to unstuck.

For inherited deployments: walk the Pre-launch Checklist now — not as a setup procedure but as a configuration audit. Most inherited deployments have at least one finding (usually stale users). Catching them on a quiet day is far better than discovering them at period close.

Pre-launch Checklist

What must be configured and verified before users can productively work in the application. Use this for first deployment, as a configuration audit when inheriting an instance, and as a periodic review at major milestones (new fiscal year, new licensed company, organizational changes).

Companies

  • Each licensed company is configured
  • The fiscal year start date matches the company’s fiscal calendar in JDE.
  • The base currency is correct.
  • AAI document types are populated and match the document types actually in use in JDE.
  • Tolerance thresholds are set to your organization’s materiality standard (or to the GSI default if you have not yet established one).
  • The company appears in the period selector with reasonable historical periods available.

Users

  • User access is appropriate
  • Every user who needs access has an account.
  • Departed staff have been removed.
  • Each user’s role matches their job (inventory accountants get a reconciliation role; A/P clerks get the A/P Clerk role; only your administrator gets RR Administrator).
  • Per-module company access is set correctly for each user.
  • At least one administrator other than yourself exists, so the system isn’t single-administrator-dependent.

Operational Readiness

  • The application is ready for end-users
  • The nightly JDE-to-RapidReconciler import has run successfully at least once.
  • Both status lights on the Reconciliation page are green for the periods end-users will work with.
  • A representative end-user from each role family (inventory, A/P) has logged in, completed their Start Here or Start Here orientation, and confirmed they can see what they need to see.
  • End-users know how to contact you (or the support inbox) when something doesn’t look right.

What to Do When the Checklist Surfaces a Problem

If a company is missing or misconfigured

Resolve through Company Management (for already-licensed companies) or Adding a Company License (if the company is not yet licensed).

If user access is wrong

Edit the affected user(s) in Manage the Team. For departed staff, deactivate or remove rather than just clearing module access — this keeps the active user list clean.

If the nightly import has not run or produced errors

This is operational rather than configuration. Contact the team that runs the import jobs (typically IT or the JDE administration team). RapidReconciler administrators do not own the import schedule itself.

Try this now

The single highest-value pre-launch action is to log in as a representative end-user (or have one of your end-users log in on a call with you) and verify that they can see what they should see.

Specifically: do their account filters show the companies and business units they need? Do they see the modules they need (Inventory, In Transit, PO Receipts) and not the ones they don’t? If they look at a closed period, do the GL Balance values match what JDE shows for the same accounts?

Most pre-launch surprises surface in the first five minutes of a real end-user logging in. Better to find them now than at period close.

Your administrator surface leads with the RapidReconciler AI band — suggested questions it answers from your own instance — over four cards you act on: Licensing, Team & access, Report Engine, and Activity log. People & Licensing

Two things sit near the top of your admin surface: who can use RapidReconciler (the Team & access card) and the state of your license (the Licensing card). Both come down to your agreement with GSI and your team.

RR Team — Your People

Open RR Team to see everyone who can sign in. For each person you decide what they can do — which areas they see, and which companies’ numbers they’re allowed to look at. A reviewer in one division never sees another division’s figures unless you grant it.

The step-by-step for adding people, setting roles, and removing access lives in Manage the Team.

Licensing — Your Company Seats

Your agreement with GSI sets how many company books you can reconcile — a fixed number of company seats on most licenses, or unlimited companies on others. The Licensing card leads with your license status and renewal date, then shows where you stand at a glance:

The full company setup — fiscal start dates, currencies, thresholds — is in Managing Companies. If you need to reconcile more companies than your seats allow, that’s a quick request to GSI: see Request a License.

What good looks like

Everyone who needs RapidReconciler can sign in and sees exactly the companies they should — no more, no less — and your seats-in-use is at or below what you’re licensed for.

Data & Refresh

RapidReconciler works from a copy of your JD Edwards figures. Keeping that copy current — and freeing up space when the history gets long — runs from the Data refresh and Purge pages. How old that copy is shows in the data freshness pill in the Home workbar, for every role; storage surfaces as a chip on the quiet instance-health strip.

The Nightly Refresh

Every night, RapidReconciler pulls the day’s changes from JD Edwards automatically. You don’t schedule it or babysit it — the data freshness pill simply tells you when it last ran, and says so plainly if it did not finish.

Reviewing the Job Schedule

The nightly refresh time is set by your on-site DBA, not by GSI — they change it at your request. The Review Job Schedule page shows the current nightly time, when it last ran, and when it runs next, so you can confirm it’s still right. There’s nothing to edit on the page; if the timing needs to move, click Request a change to draft an email you address to your DBA.

Set a 30- or 60-day reminder (or turn reminders off with Never) to keep that confirmation current. The reminder turns amber when a review is due and green once you’ve confirmed — the same gentle nudge as the Complex Passwords review, with nothing to attest to.

“Refresh now” During Close

At period close you often can’t wait for tonight. Click Refresh now to pull today’s changes immediately, so what you’re reconciling matches what was just posted in JD Edwards.

Purging Old Data

Over years, the saved history grows and takes up space. Purge old data moves the start date of the data forward: everything before that date is dropped on the next refresh, and the database gets smaller. You’re not deleting anything in JD Edwards — only RapidReconciler’s working copy of the old periods.

!
Pick the date deliberately

Choose a start date you’re sure you’ll never need to reconcile back to — closed and audited years are the usual choice. Once the next refresh runs, the periods before the new start date are gone from RapidReconciler (still in JD Edwards). If unsure, set it back a comfortable margin.

Knowing When to Purge

You don’t have to guess at timing. The Purge page shows a recommendation based on how much free disk space the server has, how fast the database is growing, and how much history you’re keeping:

  • Green — nothing to do; there’s comfortable runway.
  • Amber — worth planning a purge before long (for example, only a few months of disk space left at the current growth rate, or you’re holding far more history than a reconciliation normally needs).
  • Red — recommended now, because the server is genuinely low on free disk space.

It’s only a suggestion — you decide whether and when. If now isn’t the time, use Remind me in 1, 3, or 6 months and the recommendation steps aside until that date.

A Record of Every Change

Every start-date change is kept under Purge history: who made it, the old and new start dates, when it was made, and — importantly — when the purge actually ran. Because moving the start date forward only takes effect on the next refresh, a change reads Pending next refresh until then; a backward move reads Restore (nothing is deleted). It’s a clear audit trail of what was dropped and when.

System Health

Three of your Home surfaces tell you the engine is healthy: the Report Engine card, the Activity log card, and the AI assistant. Each carries a status you can read at a glance, so a glance tells you whether anything needs attention. On a normal day they sit green and you won’t touch them.

Report Engine

Behind RapidReconciler is a small data service — the engine that builds your reports and exports. The Report Engine card shows status, a memory bar, and a Restart button for the rare occasion when reports feel slow or won’t finish.

You seeIt meansDo
Green dot, short barHealthy, with plenty of memory headroom.Nothing.
Amber, longer barMemory has been climbing under load for a little while — it may slow down.Finish what’s running, then Restart when convenient.
Reports stuck / failingThe engine may be wedged.Click Restart.

Restarting is safe: the engine stops and comes back in a few seconds, nothing is lost, and your data is untouched — the “turn it off and on again” of RapidReconciler. A brief amber that clears on its own is normal under load; the card only flags it after the pressure has held for a couple of minutes, so it won’t chase you with false alarms.

AI Assistant

Optional AI help, powered by Claude. It is off by default, and you decide — per feature — how much it may use, from public help docs only up to your actual data. Nothing is sent until you turn a feature on, and your purchased plan caps how far the options go.

!
Read this before you opt in

Because the higher levels can send reconciliation data to Anthropic’s AI service, the full picture — exactly what leaves your server at each level, where it goes, and Anthropic’s data commitments — is on its own page: AI Assistant & Your Data.

Activity Log

A running record of recent system events — service restarts, data reloads, and scheduled refreshes — so you can see what happened and when. Set a review reminder (7, 14, or 30 days) to keep the row’s dot green; an amber dot just means a review is due, not that anything is wrong.

AI Assistant & Your Data

The AI Assistant is an optional feature that uses Claude, the AI model from Anthropic, to explain reconciliation findings and answer how-to questions in plain English.

!
Nothing is sent until you turn it on

Every feature starts at Off. No reconciliation data leaves your server unless an administrator chooses a level above Off for a specific feature and saves it. Your purchased plan sets the highest level available.

Where your data goes

Your browser → GSI service (holds the key) → Anthropic API (Claude) → answer back

The AI key lives on GSI’s server, never in your browser. When a feature is on, RapidReconciler hands the request to GSI’s service, which relays it to Anthropic’s commercial API over an encrypted connection and returns the answer. GSI does not keep your prompts beyond the moment it takes to pass them along.

What each level sends

LevelWhat leaves your server
OffNothing. The feature is hidden.
Docs-groundedOnly your typed question. None of your data — answers come from the public RapidReconciler help library.
Scrubbed analysisThe numbers and the pattern, with identifiers (account, company, document numbers, names) removed before anything is sent.
Full dataThe actual rows, so the answer is specific. The strongest disclosure — review with GSI before enabling.

Anthropic’s data commitments

When a request reaches Anthropic’s commercial API, these terms apply. Confirm the current details at the official links below.

  • Not used to train models. By default, Anthropic does not use commercial API inputs or outputs to train its models. (RapidReconciler never sends “feedback” on answers, so the feedback-based training exception does not apply.)
  • Short retention. Anthropic deletes API inputs and outputs within roughly 30 days by default. Content flagged for a policy violation may be kept longer (up to two years, and safety scores up to seven) — ordinary reconciliation questions are not flagged.
  • Zero-Data-Retention is available. An organization can request a Zero-Data-Retention arrangement, under which inputs and outputs are not stored at all except where the law requires. If your policies need this, ask GSI.
  • Security & processing. Anthropic processes data in the United States and publishes its certifications and Data Processing terms in its Trust Center (linked below).

What GSI adds on top

  • Off by default, per feature. You opt each feature in deliberately; nothing is implicit.
  • The key never reaches your browser. Requests are relayed server-to-server, so the AI credential is never exposed on an end-user’s machine.
  • Scrubbing happens before data leaves. At the Scrubbed level, identifiers are stripped on your side first — the AI service only ever sees redacted figures.
  • Your plan caps the ceiling. The highest level you can choose is set by your purchased entitlement.
i
A sensible way to start

Many teams begin with Docs-grounded help (none of your data is sent) and Scrubbed analysis, and reserve Full data for a deliberate decision made after reviewing these terms with GSI and their own security team.

Verify it yourself

These are Anthropic’s own pages — current as you read them:

  • Is my data used for training? — privacy.claude.com
  • How long is data stored? — privacy.claude.com

Utilities

The administrator has a small set of repair and maintenance tools for the rare times a reconciliation difference won’t clear by the normal steps — plus one for housekeeping. Each opens its own page: Reload GL Data, Reload Cardex Data, Correct a Period-End Date, and Purge Old Data. None of them touch JD Edwards, and you’ll go whole months without needing any of them.

!
Your JD Edwards data is never changed

Neither tool writes to JD Edwards, and neither pulls from it on the spot. Each one clears RapidReconciler’s working copy from the date you pick; the data comes back on the next scheduled refresh. Because a refresh loads the JDE system, refreshes only run on the schedule.

Reload GL Data

Clears RapidReconciler’s copy of the GL from the earliest broken period forward when a roll-forward break won’t clear by posting the open batches; the next refresh re-pulls it clean. It has its own page, so it gets its own topic: see Reload GL Data.

Reload Cardex Data

Clears RapidReconciler’s copy of the perpetual cardex from a date you choose when an item’s cardex won’t tie to its transactions; the next refresh re-pulls it clean. It has its own page, so it gets its own topic: see Reload Cardex Data.

Correct a Period-End Date

A rare one — for after a fiscal-calendar change moves a period-ending date. It has its own guarded Check → Preview → Apply flow, so it gets its own topic: see Correct a Period-End Date.

Purge Old Data

Housekeeping, not a repair. Over the years the saved history grows and takes up space. Purge Old Data (its own page) moves the start date of RapidReconciler’s data forward: everything before that date is dropped on the next refresh, and the database gets smaller. You’re not deleting anything in JD Edwards — only RapidReconciler’s working copy of the old periods. There’s a fuller walkthrough under The Nightly Refresh.

!
When to reach for these

These are last-resort repairs, not routine steps. Try the normal fix first — post the unposted GL batches, finish the day’s end-of-day run. If the difference still won’t clear, a reload gives RapidReconciler a clean copy to rebuild from. If you’re unsure which date to choose, contact RR Support before reloading.

Reload GL Data

One of the admin repair tools — you reach for it only when a general-ledger roll-forward break won’t clear by posting the open batches. It has its own page, so it lives in its own topic here rather than under Utilities.

What It Does

Reload GL clears RapidReconciler’s copy of the general ledger from the earliest broken period forward. It only ever touches RapidReconciler’s working copy — the data comes back clean on the next scheduled refresh, and your JD Edwards data is never changed.

!
Your JD Edwards data is never changed

Reload GL clears RapidReconciler’s working copy only. Nothing is written to JD Edwards, and nothing is pulled from it on the spot — the GL re-pulls on the next scheduled refresh.

When to Use It

  • Reach for it only when the account doesn’t tie out and the usual fix has already been done — that is, you’ve posted the unposted GL batches and the roll-forward break still won’t clear.
  • This is a last-resort repair, not a routine step. On a normal day you never need it.
  • If you’re unsure which date it would reload from, contact RR Support before you run it.

How It Works

The status dot on the row tells you whether there’s anything to do:

  • Green — nothing to reload; the GL roll-forward is clean.
  • Amber — a break exists that a reload can clear. The row names the date it would reload from and how many rows are affected.

Click Reload…, review the date and row count, tick the confirmation, and click Reload GL. The copy is cleared and rebuilds on the next refresh.

Guardrails

  • Try the normal fix first. Post the unposted GL batches; only reach for a reload if the break still won’t clear.
  • JD Edwards is never touched — as with every admin repair tool, only RapidReconciler’s working copy changes.
  • The GL doesn’t come back until the next scheduled refresh runs, because a refresh loads the JD Edwards system and only runs on the schedule.
  • If you’re unsure, contact RR Support before you reload.

Reload Cardex Data

One of the admin repair tools — you reach for it only when an item’s perpetual cardex (F4111) won’t tie to its transactions and the variance won’t clear. It has its own page, so it lives in its own topic here rather than under Utilities.

What It Does

Reload Cardex clears RapidReconciler’s copy of the perpetual cardex and its transaction detail from a date you choose forward — the same job as the older V7 “cardex deletions” utility. It only ever touches RapidReconciler’s working copy; the data comes back on the next scheduled refresh, and your JD Edwards data is never changed.

!
Your JD Edwards data is never changed

Reload Cardex clears RapidReconciler’s working copy only. Nothing is written to JD Edwards, and nothing is pulled from it on the spot — the cardex re-pulls on the next scheduled refresh.

When to Use It

  • Reach for it only when an item’s cardex won’t tie to its transactions and the variance won’t clear by the normal steps.
  • This is a last-resort repair, not a routine step. You’ll go whole months without needing it.
  • The reload date is your call — it isn’t calculated for you — so if you’re unsure which date to roll back to, contact RR Support before you run it.

How It Works

Click Reload…, then pick the cardex date to roll back to. The dialog shows how many cardex rows will reload. Tick the confirmation and click Reload cardex. As with the GL, JD Edwards is untouched and the cardex returns on the next scheduled refresh.

Guardrails

  • Choose the date deliberately. The reload rolls the cardex back to the date you pick, so choose the earliest date the variance reaches and no further.
  • JD Edwards is never touched — as with every admin repair tool, only RapidReconciler’s working copy changes.
  • The cardex doesn’t come back until the next scheduled refresh runs, because a refresh loads the JD Edwards system and only runs on the schedule.
  • If you’re unsure which date to choose, contact RR Support before you reload.

Correct a Period-End Date

One of the admin repair tools, and the rarest — you reach for it only after a fiscal-calendar change moves a period-ending date. It has its own guarded three-step flow, so it lives in its own topic here rather than under Utilities.

What It Does

When a fiscal-calendar change re-bases a period boundary, JD Edwards picks up the new period-ending date — but RapidReconciler’s reporting tables keep the old date until you correct it. This tool replaces the old period-ending date with the corrected one everywhere it’s stored, for all companies at once. It only ever touches RapidReconciler’s working copy; your JD Edwards data is never changed.

!
Your JD Edwards data is never changed

The correction rewrites the stored period-ending date inside RapidReconciler only. Nothing is written to JD Edwards, and nothing is pulled from it on the spot.

When to Use It

  • Run it only after a confirmed fiscal-calendar change — for example, an acquisition that re-bases a period boundary and moves an ending date.
  • Load the corrected calendar first. The Check step compares against the current fiscal calendar, so the corrected date has to be in place before you run it.
  • This is not a routine step and not a repair for an ordinary out-of-balance. If a period simply won’t tie out, use Reload GL or Reload Cardex instead — this tool is only for a period-ending date that genuinely moved.

The Three Steps

  • Check for a mismatch. Compares the latest stored period-ending date against the fiscal calendar. If one no longer matches, it fills in both the old and the corrected dates for you and unlocks the correction. Until Check finds a mismatch, there’s nothing to do — the correction stays locked.
  • Preview. Shows how many records would move, by area, before anything changes. Read this to confirm the swap looks right.
  • Apply. Makes the swap in a single pass — every stored copy of the old date becomes the corrected one, across all companies.

Guardrails

  • Always Preview before you Apply. The Preview counts are your check that the correction targets the right date and a sensible number of records.
  • The correction is locked until Check finds a mismatch, so you can’t apply a swap the calendar doesn’t call for.
  • JD Edwards is never touched — as with every admin repair tool, only RapidReconciler’s working copy changes.
  • If you’re unsure whether a date really moved, contact RR Support before you Apply.

About Administration

Orientation for the administrator role within RapidReconciler — what it is, how it’s assigned, and how your first administrator is set up at go-live.

Overview

Administrator access comes from a user’s role. Assign someone the RR Administrator role and the Administration area appears on their Home page; users with any other role never see it. You change who is an administrator by changing their role on the RR Team page.

At go-live, GSI seeds your first RR Administrator for you — that person receives a secure link to set their own password. From there, your RR Administrator maintains the RapidReconciler configuration and invites the rest of your team.

Where to begin

If you are new to the role, work through the sections in order. If you are looking up a specific task, jump directly to the relevant section from the sidebar.

Request a License

RapidReconciler is licensed per JD Edwards company number. When the customer needs to add a new JDE company to their RapidReconciler instance, the request goes to GSI Sales. License changes are not handled by RR Support — Sales owns the entitlement, RR Support owns the running application.

RapidReconciler does not license per user seat — adding or removing users is handled directly by the administrator and does not require a license change.

What to Include in Your Email

Send a short email to gsisales@getgsi.com with the items below. Sales will reply to confirm the change is in place and to coordinate any billing follow-up.

  • Customer / instance name — so Sales can match the request to your account.
  • JDE company number(s) to add — e.g., “license for JDE company 00050” or “companies 00050 and 00060”.
  • When you need it by — especially if there’s a go-live, audit, or period-close deadline.
  • Who to copy on the response — procurement, project lead, or IT.
Quick mailto

Email gsisales@getgsi.com with the four items above.

After Sales Confirms

Once Sales replies that the new company license is in place:

  • Follow Your First Setup → If You Are Adding a Company After Initial Deployment to configure the new company in RapidReconciler.
  • Once the company is configured, the administrator can grant existing or new users access to it via Manage the Team.

Vocabulary

RapidReconciler-specific terms relevant to the administrator role. Each entry is a one-line definition plus a link to the topic that explains it in depth. Skim once for awareness; come back when a term comes up in a configuration screen and you want to remember what it meant.

AAI document type
Per-company configuration that tells RapidReconciler which JDE document types apply to inventory transactions for that company. Drives the AAI-based account derivation in the Variance Calculation. Company Management.
Database & company access
The databases a user may sign in to and, within each, the companies they can see — All licensed companies or a chosen list. Set per user on the RR Team page, on top of their role.
Role
A reusable, named bundle of access (e.g. RR Administrator, Reconciliation Analyst, A/P Clerk) that decides which modules a user can use. Each user has one role; it replaces the old per-user function/tab check-boxes. Manage the Team.
Company license
The mechanism that grants RapidReconciler access to a JDE company’s data. Licensed at the GSI level; configured at the customer level. Adding a previously-unlicensed company requires a request to GSI support. Adding a Company License.
Company seat
One of the company books your agreement with GSI lets you reconcile. The Licensing row on Home shows how many of your seats are in use, e.g. “2 of 3”. People & Licensing.
Data cutoff (purge)
The start date of the data RapidReconciler keeps. Moving it forward (“purging”) drops the older periods from RapidReconciler’s working copy on the next refresh to free up space — nothing is removed from JD Edwards. Data & Refresh.
Report engine
The engine behind RapidReconciler that builds your reports and exports. Its health (and a Restart button) lives on Home under System Health.
Fiscal year start date
Per-company configuration that tells RapidReconciler when each company’s fiscal year begins. Drives period boundaries throughout the application. Must match what JDE has for the same company.
Per-module company access
The list of companies a user can see inside a specific module (Inventory, In Transit, PO Receipts). A user can have access to different sets of companies in different modules. Configured per-user in Manage the Team.
Tolerance threshold
Per-company materiality threshold used to filter the Transactions page so end-users see only meaningful variances rather than sub-unit rounding noise. Differences below the threshold are excluded by default but still roll up into the Transactions line of the headline variance.
